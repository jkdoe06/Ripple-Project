## 1. Financial Planning Foundations

### The Financial Planning Process
1. Determine current financial situation
2. Develop financial goals (SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)
3. Identify alternative courses of action
4. Evaluate alternatives
5. Create and implement a plan
6. Review and revise

### Key Financial Statements

**Balance Sheet (Net Worth Statement):**
```
Assets − Liabilities = Net Worth
```

**Income Statement (Cash Flow):**
```
Income − Expenses = Surplus/Deficit
```

### Financial Ratios

| Ratio | Formula | Target |
|-------|---------|--------|
| Savings ratio | Savings / Gross income | ≥ 10-15% |
| Debt ratio | Total liabilities / Total assets | < 50% |
| Liquidity ratio | Liquid assets / Monthly expenses | 3-6 months |
| Debt service ratio | Monthly debt payments / Gross monthly income | < 36% |

## 2. Time Value of Money

### Core Formulas

**Future Value (lump sum):**
FV = PV × (1 + r)ⁿ

**Present Value (lump sum):**
PV = FV / (1 + r)ⁿ

**Future Value of Annuity:**
FVA = PMT × [((1 + r)ⁿ - 1) / r]

**Present Value of Annuity:**
PVA = PMT × [(1 - (1 + r)⁻ⁿ) / r]

**Perpetuity:**
PV = PMT / r

### Rule of 72
Time to double your money ≈ 72 / annual interest rate

| Rate | Years to Double |
|------|----------------|
| 4% | 18 years |
| 6% | 12 years |
| 8% | 9 years |
| 10% | 7.2 years |
| 12% | 6 years |

### Real vs. Nominal Returns
Real return ≈ Nominal return − Inflation rate

**Fisher Equation (exact):**
(1 + nominal) = (1 + real) × (1 + inflation)

## 3. Investment Fundamentals

### Asset Classes

| Asset | Risk | Expected Return | Liquidity |
|-------|------|----------------|-----------|
| Cash/Money market | Very low | 1-3% | Very high |
| Bonds (govt) | Low | 3-5% | High |
| Bonds (corporate) | Low-Medium | 4-7% | Medium-High |
| Large-cap stocks | Medium | 8-12% | High |
| Small-cap stocks | High | 10-15% | Medium |
| Real estate | Medium-High | 8-12% | Low |
| Crypto/alternatives | Very high | Variable | Variable |

### Stock Valuation

**Dividend Discount Model:**
P₀ = D₁ / (r - g)
- D₁ = next year's dividend
- r = required return
- g = dividend growth rate

**P/E Ratio:**
Price per share / Earnings per share
- High P/E → growth stock (or overvalued)
- Low P/E → value stock (or declining)

### Bond Basics

**Bond pricing:** PV of all coupons + PV of face value
**Current yield:** Annual coupon / Market price
**Yield to maturity (YTM):** IRR of bond cash flows

**Bond price relationships:**
- Price ↑ when rates ↓ (inverse relationship)
- Longer maturity → more price sensitivity to rate changes
- Lower coupon → more price sensitivity to rate changes

## 4. Portfolio Theory

### Diversification
"Don't put all your eggs in one basket"

**Portfolio return:** Rp = w₁R₁ + w₂R₂ + ... + wₙRₙ

**Portfolio risk:** σp² = w₁²σ₁² + w₂²σ₂² + 2w₁w₂σ₁σ₂ρ₁₂

**Key insight:** As long as correlation (ρ) < 1, diversification reduces risk.

### Efficient Frontier
- Set of portfolios that offer the highest return for each level of risk
- Rational investors choose portfolios ON the efficient frontier
- Adding risk-free asset creates the Capital Market Line (CML)

### CAPM (Capital Asset Pricing Model)
E(Rᵢ) = Rf + βᵢ × (E(Rm) - Rf)

- Rf = risk-free rate
- βᵢ = beta (systematic risk)
- E(Rm) - Rf = market risk premium (~5-7% historically)

**Beta interpretation:**
| Beta | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| β = 0 | No market risk (like cash) |
| β = 0.5 | Half as volatile as market |
| β = 1 | Moves with market |
| β = 1.5 | 50% more volatile than market |
| β = -0.5 | Moves opposite to market |

### Sharpe Ratio
Sharpe = (Rp - Rf) / σp

Higher = better risk-adjusted return. Compare funds using Sharpe, not raw return.

## 5. Tax Planning

### 2026 Federal Tax Brackets (Single)

| Taxable Income | Rate |
|---------------|------|
| $0 - $11,925 | 10% |
| $11,926 - $48,475 | 12% |
| $48,476 - $103,350 | 22% |
| $103,351 - $197,300 | 24% |
| $197,301 - $250,525 | 32% |
| $250,526 - $626,350 | 35% |
| $626,351+ | 37% |

### Marginal vs. Effective Tax Rate
- **Marginal:** Rate on the next dollar earned
- **Effective:** Total tax / Total income (always lower than marginal)

### Key Tax Concepts

| Concept | Reduces | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Tax deduction | Taxable income | Student loan interest, 401k contribution |
| Tax credit | Tax owed (dollar for dollar) | Education credits, child tax credit |
| Tax-deferred | Tax now (pay later) | Traditional IRA, 401k |
| Tax-exempt | Tax ever | Roth IRA, municipal bonds |

### Capital Gains
- **Short-term** (held < 1 year): taxed as ordinary income
- **Long-term** (held ≥ 1 year): taxed at 0%, 15%, or 20%
- **Tax-loss harvesting:** Sell losers to offset gains

## 6. Retirement Planning

### Account Types

| Account | Tax Treatment | 2026 Limit |
|---------|--------------|------------|
| Traditional 401(k) | Pre-tax contribution, taxed at withdrawal | $24,000 |
| Roth 401(k) | After-tax contribution, tax-free withdrawal | $24,000 |
| Traditional IRA | May be pre-tax, taxed at withdrawal | $7,000 |
| Roth IRA | After-tax, tax-free growth & withdrawal | $7,000 |
| HSA | Triple tax advantage | $4,300 (individual) |

### Which Account When?

| Situation | Best Account |
|-----------|-------------|
| Low tax bracket now, expect higher later | Roth |
| High tax bracket now, expect lower later | Traditional |
| Employer match available | 401(k) up to match first |
| Young, low income | Roth IRA (tax-free growth for decades) |

### The 4% Rule
You can withdraw 4% of your retirement savings in year 1, then adjust for inflation.

**Retirement number = Annual expenses × 25**
- Need $50,000/year → need $1,250,000 saved

## 7. Insurance & Risk Management

### Types of Insurance

| Type | What It Covers | Who Needs It |
|------|---------------|-------------|
| Health | Medical expenses | Everyone |
| Auto | Vehicle damage, liability | Car owners |
| Renters/Home | Personal property, liability | Everyone |
| Life (term) | Income replacement at death | People with dependents |
| Disability | Income replacement if unable to work | Income earners |
| Umbrella | Extra liability coverage | High net worth |

### Insurance Principles
- **Insure against catastrophic risk, not minor losses**
- Higher deductible → lower premium (self-insure small stuff)
- Never insure what you can afford to lose
- Life insurance need decreases as wealth increases

## 8. Behavioral Finance

### Common Biases

| Bias | Description | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| Loss aversion | Losses hurt 2x more than equivalent gains | Holding losers too long |
| Anchoring | Over-relying on first piece of info | Buying because price was "higher before" |
| Confirmation | Seeking info that confirms beliefs | Only reading bullish analysis |
| Overconfidence | Overestimating ability | Frequent trading (costs eat returns) |
| Herd behavior | Following the crowd | Buying at market peaks |
| Mental accounting | Treating money differently by source | Spending "found money" freely |
| Recency bias | Overweighting recent events | Chasing last year's hot fund |
| Sunk cost | Continuing because of past investment | Keeping a bad stock because "I've already lost so much" |

### How to Counter Biases
1. Automate investing (remove emotion)
2. Write an investment policy statement BEFORE investing
3. Rebalance on a schedule, not on feelings
4. Diversify (protects against overconfidence)
5. Think in terms of total portfolio, not individual positions

## 9. Real Estate & Alternative Investments

### Rent vs. Buy

| Factor | Rent | Buy |
|--------|------|-----|
| Upfront cost | Low (deposit) | High (down payment + closing) |
| Monthly cost | Rent (increases) | Mortgage (fixed if fixed-rate) |
| Tax benefit | None | Mortgage interest deduction |
| Flexibility | High | Low |
| Equity building | None | Yes |
| Maintenance | Landlord's problem | Your problem |

### Real Estate Returns
- **Appreciation:** ~3-5% annually (long-term average)
- **Rental income:** 4-8% gross yield
- **Leverage:** Mortgage amplifies returns (and risk)
- **Tax benefits:** Depreciation, 1031 exchange, mortgage interest deduction

## 10. Exam Review & Key Formulas

### Must-Know Formulas
```
FV = PV(1+r)^n
PV = FV/(1+r)^n
FVA = PMT × [((1+r)^n - 1)/r]
PVA = PMT × [(1-(1+r)^(-n))/r]
Perpetuity PV = PMT/r
Rule of 72: years = 72/rate
CAPM: E(R) = Rf + β(Rm - Rf)
Sharpe = (Rp - Rf)/σp
Portfolio return = Σ(wi × Ri)
4% rule: Need = Annual expenses × 25
Real return ≈ Nominal - Inflation
```

### Common Exam Mistakes
1. Using wrong rate (monthly vs. annual) in TVM calculations
2. Confusing marginal vs. effective tax rate
3. Forgetting that Roth contributions are after-tax
4. Mixing up tax deduction (reduces income) vs. credit (reduces tax)
5. Ignoring inflation in retirement calculations
